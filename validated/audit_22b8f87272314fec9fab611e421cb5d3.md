### Title
`checkPreferTheirsOverOurs` Uses `emptyPerasWeightSnapshot` While Actual Chain Selection Uses Real Peras Weights, Causing Incorrect Peer Disconnection - (File: `ouroboros-consensus/src/ouroboros-consensus/Ouroboros/Consensus/MiniProtocol/ChainSync/Client.hs`)

---

### Summary

`checkPreferTheirsOverOurs` in the ChainSync client evaluates whether a peer's candidate chain is preferable to the local selection using a hardcoded `emptyPerasWeightSnapshot` (zero Peras weight). The actual chain selection engine (`chainSelectionForBlock`, `chainSelSync`) reads the live `PerasWeightSnapshot` from `PerasCertDB`. When Peras is active and boosted blocks exist, a peer serving a chain that is shorter by block count but heavier by total Peras weight will be incorrectly disconnected with `CandidateTooSparse`, even though the actual chain selection would have preferred that chain. The node then remains on a non-canonical, lighter chain.

---

### Finding Description

`checkPreferTheirsOverOurs` is invoked when a peer's latest header is beyond the forecast horizon. Its purpose is to disconnect from peers whose candidate chain can never be preferred over the local selection. The check calls `preferAnchoredCandidate` with a hardcoded `emptyPerasWeightSnapshot`:

```haskell
-- ChainSync/Client.hs lines 1838–1844
shouldSwitch $
  preferAnchoredCandidate
    (configBlock cfg)
    -- TODO: remove this entire check, see https://github.com/tweag/cardano-peras/issues/64
    emptyPerasWeightSnapshot
    ourFrag
    theirFrag
```

When `emptyPerasWeightSnapshot` is passed, `preferAnchoredCandidate` falls into the `isEmptyPerasWeightSnapshot weights` branch and compares chains purely by block count (`SelectView` / `BlockNo`):

```haskell
-- AnchoredFragment.hs lines 186–203
preferAnchoredCandidate cfg weights ours cand
  | isEmptyPerasWeightSnapshot weights =
      ...
      (_ :> ourTip, _ :> theirTip) ->
        case preferCandidate
          (projectChainOrderConfig cfg)
          (selectView cfg (getHeader1 ourTip))
          (selectView cfg (getHeader1 theirTip)) of ...
```

In contrast, the actual chain selection in `chainSelectionForBlock` reads the live weight snapshot from `PerasCertDB`:

```haskell
-- ChainSel.hs lines 629–634
(invalid, curChain, weights) <-
  atomically $
    (,,)
      <$> (forgetFingerprint <$> readTVar cdbInvalid)
      <*> Query.getCurrentChain cdb
      <*> (forgetFingerprint <$> Query.getPerasWeightSnapshot cdb)
```

`Query.getPerasWeightSnapshot` reads from `PerasCertDB.getWeightSnapshot`, which contains real Peras certificate boosts. The same real weights are used in `chainSelSync` (line 382) and in `constructPreferableCandidates` (line 777).

The divergence is exact: `checkPreferTheirsOverOurs` checks with zero weights; the chain selection engine decides with non-zero weights. This is the direct analog of the external report's pattern — a check is performed on an intermediate/wrong value, while the actual outcome uses a different value.

---

### Impact Explanation

**Impact: High** — Chain selection bug that causes an honest node to prefer a non-canonical chain.

When Peras is active and a peer's chain contains Peras-boosted blocks:

1. Peer serves a chain with 9 blocks but with a large Peras weight boost (e.g., total weight = 209).
2. Our chain has 10 blocks but no Peras boost (total weight = 10).
3. Peer's latest header is beyond the forecast horizon → `checkPreferTheirsOverOurs` fires.
4. With `emptyPerasWeightSnapshot`: their 9 blocks < our 10 blocks → `ShouldNotSwitch` → `throwSTM CandidateTooSparse` → peer disconnected.
5. With real weights: their 209 > our 10 → `ShouldSwitch` → chain selection would have adopted their chain.

The node stays on the lighter, non-canonical chain. The canonical chain (the one with Peras weight boosts) is never adopted because the only peer serving it was disconnected. This violates the Peras chain selection invariant that the heaviest chain should be preferred.

---

### Likelihood Explanation

**Likelihood: Medium.**

- Peras is an active extension under development and deployment in this codebase; the `PerasWeightSnapshot` infrastructure, `PerasCertDB`, and `addPerasCertAsync` are all production code paths.
- The scenario requires a peer's chain to be shorter by block count but heavier by Peras weight — a realistic scenario when Peras certificates boost a fork that has not yet grown as long as the local chain.
- The developers have already identified this as a known issue: the TODO comment at line 1841 explicitly says `"TODO: remove this entire check, see https://github.com/tweag/cardano-peras/issues/64"`, confirming the check is incorrect in the Peras context.
- The trigger condition (header beyond forecast horizon) is a normal network condition, not an exotic one.

---

### Recommendation

Replace the hardcoded `emptyPerasWeightSnapshot` in `checkPreferTheirsOverOurs` with the live `PerasWeightSnapshot` read from the ChainDB (the same snapshot used by `chainSelectionForBlock`). The ChainSync client already has access to the ChainDB handle; `getPerasWeightSnapshot` can be called in STM alongside the existing `getCurrentChain` read. Alternatively, as the TODO suggests, remove the check entirely and rely on the GDD/LoP mechanisms to handle the sparse-chain case, which are already Peras-weight-aware.

---

### Proof of Concept

**Setup**: Peras is active. Our chain has 10 blocks, no Peras boosts. A peer's chain has 9 blocks, with a Peras certificate boosting block 5 by weight 200 (total weight = 209 vs. our 10).

**Trigger sequence**:
1. Peer sends headers up to block 9. Block 9's slot is beyond the forecast horizon.
2. `projectLedgerView` returns `Nothing` (slot outside forecast range).
3. `checkPreferTheirsOverOurs` is called with `ourFrag` (10 blocks) and `theirFrag` (9 blocks).
4. `preferAnchoredCandidate cfg emptyPerasWeightSnapshot ourFrag theirFrag` compares `selectView` of tip 10 vs. tip 9 → `ShouldNotSwitch GT` (our block 10 > their block 9).
5. `throwSTM CandidateTooSparse` → peer disconnected.
6. `chainSelectionForBlock` is never reached for this peer's chain.
7. Node remains on its 10-block chain (weight 10) instead of adopting the peer's 9-block chain (weight 209).
