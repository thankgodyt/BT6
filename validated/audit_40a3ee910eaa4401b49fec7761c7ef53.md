Audit Report

## Title
`claim_fee` Gated by `#[trusted_relayer]` Combined with `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id` Permanently Freezes Relayer Fees - (File: `near/omni-bridge/src/lib.rs`)

## Summary
The `claim_fee` function is guarded by `#[trusted_relayer]`, restricting callers to accounts that currently hold trusted-relayer status. A second check in `claim_fee_callback` requires `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id`, meaning only the exact NEAR account encoded in the foreign-chain proof can trigger fee delivery — and only while they remain a trusted relayer. If a relayer resigns or is revoked after finalizing a transfer, their earned fees are permanently frozen in the bridge contract with no recovery path.

## Finding Description
`claim_fee` at line 1054–1064 of `near/omni-bridge/src/lib.rs` carries `#[trusted_relayer]` at the function level:

```rust
#[payable]
#[trusted_relayer]
#[pause(except(roles(Role::DAO)))]
pub fn claim_fee(&mut self, #[serializer(borsh)] args: ClaimFeeArgs) -> Promise {
```

The impl block at lines 245–249 configures `bypass_roles(Role::DAO, Role::UnrestrictedRelayer)` for the macro, but the `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id` check in `claim_fee_callback` (lines 1083–1086) is a hard runtime require that no bypass role can circumvent — it compares the proof-encoded `fee_recipient` against the actual caller:

```rust
require!(
    fee_recipient == *predecessor_account_id,
    BridgeError::OnlyFeeRecipientCanClaim.as_ref()
);
```

The combined effect:
- **Gate 1 (`#[trusted_relayer]`):** Only a currently-active trusted relayer can call `claim_fee`. A DAO account with `bypass_roles` can pass this gate.
- **Gate 2 (`fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id`):** Only the exact account encoded as `fee_recipient` in the cryptographic proof can pass this check. A DAO account calling on behalf of a resigned relayer will always fail here because `dao.near ≠ relayer.near`.

The `resign_trusted_relayer` function is a supported, tested protocol operation (confirmed in `near/omni-tests/src/relayer_staking.rs` lines 293–368). A relayer who resigns after finalizing a transfer loses trusted-relayer status immediately, and their `fee_recipient`-encoded fees become permanently unclaimable.

## Impact Explanation
The fee amount (`transfer_message.amount.0 - denormalized_amount` at line 1131) represents real bridged tokens locked in the bridge contract. When a relayer resigns before claiming, these tokens are permanently frozen — no account, including the DAO, can route them to the correct `fee_recipient` because Gate 2 rejects any caller that is not the exact `fee_recipient`. This constitutes **permanent freezing of bridged funds**, matching the Critical impact class.

## Likelihood Explanation
Resigning as a trusted relayer after completing bridge work is a normal, non-adversarial lifecycle event. The protocol explicitly supports `resign_trusted_relayer` and returns the relayer's stake on resignation. A relayer who finalizes multiple transfers and then resigns — a completely ordinary sequence — will find all their pending fee claims permanently blocked. No attacker action is required; the relayer simply needs to resign before calling `claim_fee`.

## Recommendation
Remove the `#[trusted_relayer]` attribute from `claim_fee`. The fee destination is fixed by the cryptographically verified proof (`fee_recipient` in the foreign-chain finalization event), so allowing any caller to trigger fee delivery does not introduce a security risk — funds are always routed to the proof-encoded address. Optionally, also relax the `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id` check to allow any caller to trigger delivery to the proof-encoded `fee_recipient`, enabling third-party sweepers to unblock stuck fees on behalf of resigned relayers.

## Proof of Concept
1. Relayer R becomes a trusted relayer via `apply_for_trusted_relayer` and waits past the activation period.
2. R calls `fin_transfer` on a foreign-chain proof, finalizing a transfer with a non-zero fee and setting their NEAR account as `fee_recipient` in the foreign-chain event.
3. R calls `resign_trusted_relayer`, receiving their stake back and losing trusted-relayer status (confirmed by `is_trusted_relayer` returning `false`).
4. R calls `claim_fee` with the valid proof — the call is rejected at the `#[trusted_relayer]` gate before reaching proof verification.
5. Any other trusted relayer T calls `claim_fee` with the same proof — passes Gate 1 but fails at `require!(fee_recipient == *predecessor_account_id)` since `T ≠ R`.
6. The DAO calls `claim_fee` — passes Gate 1 via `bypass_roles` but fails Gate 2 for the same reason.
