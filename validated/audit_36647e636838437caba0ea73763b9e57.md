Audit Report

## Title
Fee Tokens Permanently Locked When `fee_recipient` Relayer Is Revoked Before Calling `claim_fee` - (File: `near/omni-bridge/src/lib.rs`)

## Summary
`claim_fee` enforces two independent caller restrictions: a `#[trusted_relayer]` guard and an explicit `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id` check in the callback. If the relayer designated as `fee_recipient` at `sign_transfer` time is subsequently revoked or resigns, neither they nor any other account can satisfy both restrictions simultaneously. The fee portion of every affected transfer is permanently frozen in the bridge contract, and `locked_tokens` is never decremented, creating a permanent accounting discrepancy.

## Finding Description
The NEAR→Foreign transfer flow stores a `TransferMessage` in `pending_transfers` with the full token amount (including fee). When a trusted relayer calls `sign_transfer`, the `fee_recipient` is embedded into the MPC-signed `TransferMessagePayload` and is cryptographically fixed. The fee remains locked on NEAR until `claim_fee` is called with a proof from the destination chain.

`claim_fee` at line 1055 carries `#[trusted_relayer]`, which is defined at the impl block level with `bypass_roles(Role::DAO, Role::UnrestrictedRelayer)` (lines 245–249). This means only currently-active trusted relayers, DAO accounts, or `UnrestrictedRelayer` accounts can pass the first gate.

Inside `claim_fee_callback` (lines 1079–1086), a second independent check requires:
```rust
require!(
    fee_recipient == *predecessor_account_id,
    BridgeError::OnlyFeeRecipientCanClaim.as_ref()
);
```

The `fee_recipient` is extracted from the prover result (the destination-chain proof), not from contract state, so it cannot be changed after `sign_transfer`. These two restrictions create a deadlock:

- The revoked relayer fails restriction 1 (`#[trusted_relayer]`).
- Any DAO or other trusted relayer passes restriction 1 but fails restriction 2 because `fee_recipient` is the revoked relayer's account ID.
- No account can satisfy both simultaneously.

The only DAO escape hatch is `transfer_token_as_dao` (lines 1511–1530), which can move tokens out of the contract but does **not** call `remove_transfer_message`, does not decrement `locked_tokens`, and does not remove the entry from `pending_transfers`. The accounting discrepancy is permanent regardless.

`sign_transfer_callback` (lines 655–658) confirms that `remove_transfer_message` is only called immediately when `fee.is_zero()`; for non-zero fees the entry persists until `claim_fee` succeeds.

## Impact Explanation
The fee portion of every NEAR→Foreign transfer whose `fee_recipient` relayer is revoked or resigns before calling `claim_fee` is permanently frozen inside the bridge contract. The `locked_tokens` counter retains a stale inflated value, causing a permanent discrepancy between the bridge's accounting and its actual spendable balance. This matches two allowed critical impact classes: **permanent freezing of bridged funds** and **fee mis-accounting that changes protocol balances**.

## Likelihood Explanation
The DAO legitimately revokes relayers for misbehavior via `reject_relayer_application`, and relayers can voluntarily resign via `resign_trusted_relayer`. Any relayer that has signed one or more transfers with a non-zero fee and has not yet called `claim_fee` will have those fees permanently locked the instant they lose trusted-relayer status. No attacker is required; this is a natural consequence of normal protocol administration. The scenario is repeatable across any number of transfers and any number of revoked relayers.

## Recommendation
Remove the `#[trusted_relayer]` guard from `claim_fee` (or add a specific bypass for the `fee_recipient` account regardless of current relayer status), so that the designated `fee_recipient` can always claim their earned fee even after losing trusted-relayer status. Alternatively, add a DAO-only admin function that can administratively clear a stuck `pending_transfers` entry, properly decrement `locked_tokens`, and transfer the fee to a configurable rescue address.

## Proof of Concept
1. User initiates a NEAR→Eth transfer with `fee = 1000` tokens. `pending_transfers` stores the `TransferMessage` with `amount = 10000`, `fee.fee = 1000`.
2. Trusted relayer `relayer.near` calls `sign_transfer(transfer_id, fee_recipient="relayer.near", fee=...)`. MPC signature is produced; destination chain finalizes, sending `9000` tokens to the user.
3. DAO calls `reject_relayer_application("relayer.near")`. `relayer.near` is no longer a trusted relayer (confirmed by `test_dao_revoke_active_relayer` in `near/omni-tests/src/relayer_staking.rs`).
4. `relayer.near` attempts `claim_fee(...)` → panics at the `#[trusted_relayer]` guard.
5. Any DAO account attempts `claim_fee(...)` → passes restriction 1 (DAO is in `bypass_roles`) but panics at `BridgeError::OnlyFeeRecipientCanClaim` because `fee_recipient ("relayer.near") != predecessor_account_id (dao.near)`.
6. The `TransferMessage` with `fee = 1000` tokens remains in `pending_transfers` indefinitely. `locked_tokens` is never decremented by the fee amount.

A sandbox integration test can reproduce this by: (a) setting up a trusted relayer, (b) initiating a transfer with non-zero fee, (c) calling `sign_transfer`, (d) calling `reject_relayer_application` via a DAO account, (e) asserting that both `relayer.near` and `dao.near` fail `claim_fee`, and (f) asserting that `get_transfer_message` still returns the entry and `locked_tokens` is unchanged.
