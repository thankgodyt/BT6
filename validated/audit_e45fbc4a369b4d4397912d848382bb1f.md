### Title
Unprivileged Attacker Can Front-Run `request_refund` to Block User's Refund and Redirect BTC to Attacker-Controlled Address - (File: `contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/api/bridge.rs`)

---

### Summary

`request_refund` is callable by any unprivileged NEAR account. A one-request-per-UTXO constraint is enforced in `request_refund_callback`. An attacker who observes a user's pending `request_refund` call (via the NEAR mempool or Bitcoin chain) can front-run it with an attacker-controlled BTC `refund_address`, permanently occupying the UTXO's refund slot and blocking the legitimate user's refund. After the 14-day `unsafe_refund_timelock_sec`, the attacker can call `execute_refund` (also unrestricted) to drain the deposit UTXO to their own Bitcoin address.

### Finding Description

`request_refund` in `bridge.rs` carries no individual `#[trusted_relayer]` attribute and no role-based access control — only `#[payable]` and `#[pause]`: [1](#0-0) 

Any NEAR account can call it by supplying a valid BTC Merkle proof (public on-chain data), the raw `tx_bytes` (also public), and any `refund_address` they choose — provided `deposit_msg.refund_address` is `None`, which is the standard case for ordinary deposits.

Inside `request_refund_callback`, a hard uniqueness guard prevents a second request for the same UTXO: [2](#0-1) 

Once the attacker's request is stored, the legitimate user's subsequent `request_refund` call reverts with `"Refund request already exists for this UTXO"`.

`execute_refund` is equally unrestricted — no role check, no `#[trusted_relayer]`: [3](#0-2)
