Audit Report

## Title
Trusted-Relayer Gate on `claim_fee` Permanently Freezes Earned Fee Tokens When Relayer Resigns or Is Revoked — (`near/omni-bridge/src/lib.rs`)

## Summary
`claim_fee` is gated by `#[trusted_relayer]`, which rejects any caller not currently in the active-relayer set. Simultaneously, `claim_fee_callback` enforces that only the account matching the `fee_recipient` embedded in the on-chain proof may collect the fee. When a relayer resigns or is revoked after executing a fast transfer to a non-NEAR destination chain but before the destination-chain proof is submitted, neither the original relayer nor any substitute can ever call `claim_fee` for that transfer. The fee tokens and the `pending_transfers` entry are permanently frozen in the bridge contract with no permissionless recovery path.

## Finding Description

**Restriction 1 — caller must be a currently-trusted relayer:**

`claim_fee` carries `#[trusted_relayer]` at line 1055. The `#[trusted_relayer]` configuration on the `impl` block at lines 245–249 defines `bypass_roles(Role::DAO, Role::UnrestrictedRelayer)`, meaning only accounts with those roles can bypass the check. A resigned or revoked relayer has neither role.

**Restriction 2 — caller must equal the `fee_recipient` in the proof:**

Inside `claim_fee_callback` (lines 1083–1086), the contract requires `fee_recipient == *predecessor_account_id`. The `fee_recipient` is cryptographically bound in the destination-chain event proof. No other account can satisfy this check. Even if the DAO calls `claim_fee` (bypassing Restriction 1 via `bypass_roles`), the DAO's account ID will not match the resigned relayer's `fee_recipient`, so Restriction 2 still panics.

**How the fee becomes stuck:**

When a trusted relayer executes a fast transfer to a non-NEAR chain, `fast_fin_transfer_to_other_chain` (lines 914–972):
- Burns/locks the principal amount
- Stores a `FastTransferStatus { relayer: RelayerA, finalised: false }` in `fast_transfers`
- Creates a second-leg `TransferMessage` in `pending_transfers` with `origin_transfer_id = Some(fast_transfer_id)` and the full `fee` still accounted for inside the bridge

The fee is only released when `claim_fee_callback` calls `send_fee_internal` (lines 2650–2701), which mints or transfers the fee tokens to the `fee_recipient`. If the relayer resigns (`resign_trusted_relayer`) or is revoked by the DAO (`reject_relayer_application`) before the destination-chain proof is available, the fee tokens remain locked in `locked_tokens` and the `pending_transfers` entry persists indefinitely.

The resign/revoke path is confirmed to immediately remove trusted status and return the stake (test at lines 336–368 of `near/omni-tests/src/relayer_staking.rs`), with no grace period for pending fee claims.

## Impact Explanation

Fee tokens earned by a relayer for executing a fast transfer to a non-NEAR destination chain are permanently frozen in the bridge contract. The `pending_transfers` entry for the second leg is never removed, constituting both fee mis-accounting and permanent freezing of bridged funds within the bridge's escrow. The only out-of-band escape is DAO intervention via a privileged admin function, which does not restore the fee to the rightful relayer and is not a permissionless recovery path. This matches the allowed impact: **fee mis-accounting / permanent freezing of bridged funds**.

## Likelihood Explanation

Low. The window requires a relayer to resign or be revoked after submitting a fast transfer but before the destination-chain proof is finalized and submitted. For chains with longer finality (e.g., Ethereum mainnet, ~12 minutes to finality), this window is realistic. The DAO revocation scenario (`reject_relayer_application` on an active relayer) is particularly credible: the DAO may revoke a relayer for misconduct while that relayer has pending fee claims, causing legitimate earned fees to be frozen as a side effect of the revocation.

## Recommendation

Remove the `#[trusted_relayer]` gate from `claim_fee`. The `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id` check in `claim_fee_callback` already ensures only the correct party can collect the fee; the additional trusted-relayer check adds no security benefit and creates the described dead-end. Alternatively, allow the `fee_recipient` to call `claim_fee` regardless of current relayer status, since the fee was earned at the time the fast transfer was executed.

## Proof of Concept

1. Relayer A applies and becomes a trusted relayer (stake deposited, waiting period elapsed).
2. Relayer A calls `ft_transfer_call` on the token contract with a `FastFinTransferMsg` targeting a Base-chain recipient. `fast_fin_transfer_to_other_chain` burns the principal, stores a second-leg `TransferMessage` in `pending_transfers` with `origin_transfer_id = Some(fast_transfer_id)`, and records `FastTransferStatus { relayer: RelayerA, finalised: false }`.
3. A regular relayer calls `sign_transfer` on the second-leg transfer. Because `fee > 0`, the `TransferMessage` remains in `pending_transfers` after signing (confirmed by `sign_transfer_callback` at lines 648–668: only removes the message if `fee.is_zero()`).
4. Relayer A calls `resign_trusted_relayer`. Stake is returned; Relayer A is removed from the trusted set.
5. The signed payload is submitted to Base. Base emits `FinTransfer` with `fee_recipient = RelayerA`.
6. Relayer A attempts `claim_fee` with the Base proof → panics at `#[trusted_relayer]` because Relayer A is no longer active.
7. Any other trusted relayer attempts `claim_fee` with the same proof → panics at `OnlyFeeRecipientCanClaim` because `fee_recipient (RelayerA) != predecessor_account_id`.
8. The DAO attempts `claim_fee` (bypassing Restriction 1 via `bypass_roles`) → panics at `OnlyFeeRecipientCanClaim` because `fee_recipient (RelayerA) != predecessor_account_id (DAO)`.
9. The fee tokens and the `pending_transfers` entry are permanently stuck. No permissionless path exists to recover them.
