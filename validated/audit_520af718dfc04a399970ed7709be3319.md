Audit Report

## Title
Blacklisted EVM Recipient Permanently Freezes Bridged Funds in `finTransfer` - (File: `evm/src/omni-bridge/contracts/OmniBridge.sol`)

## Summary
`OmniBridge.sol::finTransfer` unconditionally pushes tokens to `payload.recipient` using a push-based delivery model. Because `payload.recipient` is Borsh-encoded into the MPC-signed message, it cannot be changed without invalidating the signature. If the recipient is blacklisted by a token contract (e.g., USDC) after the NEAR side has already burned or locked the user's tokens, every finalization attempt reverts permanently with no recovery path.

## Finding Description
In `finTransfer`, the nonce is marked consumed at line 287 before the token transfer:

```solidity
completedTransfers[payload.destinationNonce] = true;
```

The recipient address is Borsh-encoded into the signed payload at line 298:

```solidity
Borsh.encodeAddress(payload.recipient),
```

The signature is verified at line 311 against `nearBridgeDerivedAddress`. Because `payload.recipient` is part of the signed message, no caller can substitute a different recipient without producing an `InvalidSignature` revert. For native ERC20 tokens (e.g., USDC held in escrow by the bridge), the transfer path at lines 351–354 is:

```solidity
IERC20(payload.tokenAddress).safeTransfer(
    payload.recipient,
    payload.amount
);
```

If `payload.recipient` is USDC-blacklisted, `safeTransfer` reverts, rolling back the entire transaction including the nonce write. The nonce is therefore not permanently consumed, but every subsequent retry with the same signed payload will also revert for the same reason. No pull-based claim function, no redirect mechanism, and no cancellation path exist anywhere in the EVM contracts (confirmed: no `claimable`, `claim.*token`, or `fallback.*recipient` patterns exist in `evm/**/*.sol`).

On the NEAR side, `init_transfer_internal` at lines 1850–1857 burns deployed bridge tokens or locks native tokens before EVM finalization is attempted. The pending transfer record in `pending_transfers` is only removed upon successful finalization. With EVM finalization permanently blocked, the NEAR-side record and the burned/locked tokens are irrecoverable.

## Impact Explanation
This constitutes **permanent freezing of bridged funds** across the NEAR→EVM flow, which is explicitly within the critical impact scope. The user's tokens are burned or locked on NEAR, and the EVM release is permanently blocked with no protocol-level recovery mechanism. The funds are not merely delayed — they are unrecoverable without a contract upgrade.

## Likelihood Explanation
USDC is one of the most commonly bridged assets. Circle regularly exercises its blacklisting capability in response to sanctions and law enforcement requests. The window between a user calling `init_transfer` on NEAR (which immediately burns/locks tokens) and a relayer calling `finTransfer` on EVM can range from minutes to hours, providing a realistic opportunity for blacklisting to occur in between. No privileged attacker action is required — the blacklisting is performed by the token issuer as part of normal operations. The scenario requires only: (1) a user bridging a blacklist-capable token, and (2) the token issuer blacklisting that user's EVM address before finalization.

## Recommendation
Replace the push-based delivery in `finTransfer` with a pull-based pattern:

1. Instead of calling `safeTransfer` directly to `payload.recipient` inside `finTransfer`, record the claimable balance in a mapping: `claimable[recipient][token] += amount`.
2. Mark the nonce as consumed and emit `FinTransfer` as normal — finalization succeeds regardless of recipient status.
3. Expose a separate `claimTokens(address token)` function that the recipient (or any address they authorize) calls to withdraw their balance.
4. This isolates a single recipient's blacklist status from the finalization of the transfer, preventing any one recipient from permanently blocking the protocol flow.

## Proof of Concept
1. Alice holds 10,000 USDC on NEAR and calls `init_transfer` targeting her EVM address. The NEAR bridge burns her USDC representation and records the pending transfer in `pending_transfers`.
2. Before any relayer calls `finTransfer` on EVM, Circle blacklists Alice's EVM address in the USDC contract.
3. A relayer calls `OmniBridge.finTransfer(signatureData, payload)` where `payload.recipient = Alice_EVM` and `payload.tokenAddress = USDC`.
4. Execution reaches line 287: `completedTransfers[payload.destinationNonce] = true`.
5. Execution reaches line 311: signature verification passes (recipient is correctly encoded).
6. Execution reaches line 351: `IERC20(USDC).safeTransfer(Alice_EVM, amount)` — USDC's `transfer` reverts because Alice is blacklisted.
7. The entire transaction reverts; the nonce write is rolled back.
8. Every future relayer attempt to finalize this transfer also reverts for the same reason, since `payload.recipient` is fixed by the MPC signature.
