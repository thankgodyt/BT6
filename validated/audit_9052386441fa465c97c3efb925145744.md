### Title
`OmniBridge` permanently locks ETH received via `receive()` and `nativeFee` with no withdrawal path — (File: `evm/src/omni-bridge/contracts/OmniBridge.sol`)

---

### Summary

`OmniBridge.sol` declares a bare `receive() external payable {}` function and accepts ETH as `nativeFee` on every `initTransfer` call, but contains no function to withdraw or recover that ETH. All ETH that enters the contract through either path is permanently locked.

---

### Finding Description

`OmniBridge` has two distinct ETH-locking paths with no corresponding withdrawal mechanism:

**Path 1 — bare `receive()` function** [1](#0-0) 

The contract exposes `receive() external payable {}` with an empty body. Any ETH sent directly to the contract address (e.g., by a user, a relayer, or another contract) is accepted and permanently trapped.

**Path 2 — `nativeFee` in `initTransfer` / `initTransfer1155`**

`initTransfer` is `payable` and requires callers to include `nativeFee` in `msg.value`: [2](#0-1) 

The function computes `extensionValue = msg.value - nativeFee` (ERC-20 path) or `msg.value - amount - nativeFee` (native ETH path) and passes only `extensionValue` to `initTransferExtension`. The `nativeFee` portion is never forwarded.

In `OmniBridgeWormhole`, `initTransferExtension` forwards only `value` (i.e., `extensionValue`) to the Wormhole core contract: [3](#0-2) 

In the base `OmniBridge`, `initTransferExtension` reverts if `value != 0`, meaning `msg.value` must equal exactly `nativeFee` (ERC-20) or `amount + nativeFee` (native ETH): [4](#0-3) 

In both cases, the `nativeFee` ETH remains in the contract after the call. A search across all EVM contracts confirms there is no `withdrawEth`, `rescueEth`, or equivalent function anywhere in the codebase.

---

### Impact Explanation

Every `initTransfer` or `initTransfer1155` call that includes a non-zero `nativeFee` permanently destroys that ETH — it is credited to the contract's balance but can never be recovered by the user, a relayer, or the protocol. Over the lifetime of the bridge this accumulates into a growing pool of irrecoverable ETH. Additionally, any ETH sent directly to the contract via `receive()` is also permanently lost. This constitutes a permanent, irreversible loss of user and protocol funds.

---

### Likelihood Explanation

The `nativeFee` parameter is a first-class field in the public `initTransfer` interface and is emitted in the `InitTransfer` event, indicating it is an expected, routine part of bridge usage. Any user or relayer who pays a `nativeFee` to incentivize cross-chain relaying will trigger the loss. No special permissions or unusual conditions are required — a standard bridge transfer is sufficient.

---

### Recommendation

1. **Remove `receive()`** if the contract is not intended to accept unsolicited ETH, or add an admin-only ETH withdrawal function.
2. **Forward `nativeFee` to the fee recipient** inside `initTransfer` (e.g., to `msg.sender`, a designated relayer address, or a fee collector), rather than leaving it in the contract balance.
3. **Add an admin ETH rescue function** (e.g., `withdrawEth(address payable to, uint256 amount) external onlyRole(DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE)`) as a safety net for any ETH that accumulates in the contract.

---

### Proof of Concept

1. Alice calls `initTransfer(usdcAddress, 1000e6, 0, 0.01 ether, "alice.near", "")` and sends `msg.value = 0.01 ether` as `nativeFee`.
2. Inside `initTransfer`, `extensionValue = 0.01 ether - 0.01 ether = 0`. The `nativeFee` is never forwarded.
3. `initTransferExtension` is called with `value = 0`; in `OmniBridgeWormhole` this calls `_wormhole.publishMessage{value: 0}(...)`.
4. The `0.01 ether` remains in `OmniBridge`'s balance.
5. No function exists to withdraw it. Alice's `nativeFee` is permanently locked. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6)
