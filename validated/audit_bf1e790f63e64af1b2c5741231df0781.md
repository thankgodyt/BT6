Audit Report

## Title
Relayer Removal Without Settling Pending Fee Claims Causes Permanent Fee Loss - (File: `near/omni-bridge/src/lib.rs`)

## Summary
The `claim_fee` entry point is gated by `#[trusted_relayer]`, and `claim_fee_callback` additionally enforces `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id`. When a relayer is removed via `resign_trusted_relayer` or `reject_relayer_application` while holding pending fee claims in `pending_transfers`, those fees become permanently unclaimable: the removed relayer is blocked by the `#[trusted_relayer]` guard, and no other account can satisfy the `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id` check. For non-deployed (locked) tokens, the fee portion remains permanently frozen inside the bridge contract.

## Finding Description
`claim_fee` at `near/omni-bridge/src/lib.rs:1054-1064` carries `#[trusted_relayer]`, which rejects callers who are not in the trusted relayer set. The impl-block-level `#[trusted_relayer]` at lines 245-249 declares `bypass_roles(Role::DAO, Role::UnrestrictedRelayer)`, but even if a DAO account bypasses the method guard, `claim_fee_callback` at lines 1083-1086 enforces:

```rust
require!(
    fee_recipient == *predecessor_account_id,
    BridgeError::OnlyFeeRecipientCanClaim.as_ref()
);
```

where `fee_recipient` is extracted from the submitted proof and must equal the caller. A DAO account calling `claim_fee` would have itself as `predecessor_account_id` but the proof would name the original relayer as `fee_recipient`, causing the check to fail. No alternative code path exists to claim fees without being both the trusted relayer and the recorded `fee_recipient`.

The relayer earns fees by calling `fin_transfer` (lines 670-696), which stores a `TransferMessage` in `pending_transfers` with the relayer as fee recipient via `process_fin_transfer_to_other_chain`. The relayer must subsequently call `claim_fee` with a destination-chain proof to collect. Neither `resign_trusted_relayer` nor `reject_relayer_application` (confirmed by `test_dao_revoke_active_relayer` at `near/omni-tests/src/relayer_staking.rs:411-491`) performs any check for outstanding `pending_transfers` entries where the relayer is the fee recipient, and no settlement step is executed before deactivation.

For non-deployed tokens, `send_fee_internal` at lines 2692-2697 calls `ft_transfer` to pay the fee from the bridge's locked balance. If `claim_fee` can never be called, `remove_transfer_message` at line 1094 is never executed, the `pending_transfers` entry persists indefinitely, and the locked token balance is never decremented — permanently freezing the fee portion of bridged funds.

## Impact Explanation
For non-deployed (locked) tokens: the fee amount remains locked inside the bridge contract forever, constituting permanent freezing of bridged funds — a Critical allowed impact. For deployed (mintable) tokens: the relayer's earned fee is never minted and the `pending_transfers` entry persists indefinitely. The `transfer_token_as_dao` escape hatch (lines 1511-1529) exists but is not a designed settlement path; it requires manual DAO identification and intervention per stuck transfer and does not remove the `pending_transfers` entry, leaving storage permanently consumed.

## Likelihood Explanation
Two realistic, unprivileged-actor-reachable paths exist:

1. **Voluntary resignation**: A relayer calls `resign_trusted_relayer` without first claiming all pending fees (e.g., after a batch of `fin_transfer` calls before destination-chain proofs are available). The relayer is an unprivileged external actor.
2. **DAO revocation**: The DAO calls `reject_relayer_application` on an active relayer (confirmed by `test_dao_revoke_active_relayer`) without checking for outstanding fee claims. The DAO receives the stake; the relayer's pending fees are frozen. The DAO is a privileged actor, but the relayer (the victim) is unprivileged and cannot prevent this outcome.

Both paths are reachable through normal public contract calls and require no unrealistic assumptions.

## Recommendation
Before removing a relayer from the trusted set, the protocol should either:
1. **Require zero pending fee claims**: Revert if any `pending_transfers` entries exist where the relayer is the fee recipient.
2. **Decouple fee claiming from trusted status**: In `claim_fee`, replace the `#[trusted_relayer]` guard with a check that `env::predecessor_account_id()` matches the `fee_recipient` recorded in the proof, independently of trusted status. The existing `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id` check in `claim_fee_callback` already provides the necessary authorization.
3. **Settle pending claims first**: Automatically process outstanding fee claims for the relayer before deactivation.

## Proof of Concept
1. Relayer R becomes trusted via `apply_for_trusted_relayer` + waiting period.
2. R calls `fin_transfer` for a cross-chain transfer (non-NEAR recipient); `fin_transfer_callback` stores a `TransferMessage` in `pending_transfers` with R as fee recipient via `process_fin_transfer_to_other_chain`.
3. R calls `resign_trusted_relayer` (or DAO calls `reject_relayer_application` on R). R is removed from the trusted set.
4. R attempts to call `claim_fee` with the destination-chain proof.
5. The `#[trusted_relayer]` macro at line 1055 rejects the call because R is no longer trusted.
6. No other account can call `claim_fee` for this transfer: the `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id` check at lines 1083-1086 blocks any account other than R, and R is blocked by step 5.
7. `remove_transfer_message` is never called; the fee tokens remain permanently locked in the bridge; the `pending_transfers` entry is never removed.

Reproducible as an integration test extending `test_dao_revoke_active_relayer` in `near/omni-tests/src/relayer_staking.rs`: add a `fin_transfer` call before revocation, then assert that `claim_fee` fails after revocation and that the `pending_transfers` entry still exists.
