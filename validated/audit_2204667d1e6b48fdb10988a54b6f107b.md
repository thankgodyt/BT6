### Title
DoS on Withdrawal Due to Excessively Tight `max_btc_gas_fee` Constraint - (File: `contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/psbt.rs`)

### Summary
The `check_withdraw_psbt` function enforces a hard upper bound on the Bitcoin gas fee (`max_btc_gas_fee`). When Bitcoin network fees spike above this configured ceiling, all withdrawal PSBTs are rejected. A user who already has a pending withdrawal (nBTC locked in the bridge) cannot RBF their transaction to a higher fee — the same constraint blocks RBF — and cannot self-cancel. The bridge enters a stuck state requiring DAO/Operator intervention.

### Finding Description
In `check_withdraw_psbt`, after computing `gas_fee = total_input_amount - total_output_amount`, the contract enforces:

```rust
require!(
    gas_fee >= config.min_btc_gas_fee && gas_fee <= config.max_btc_gas_fee,
    format!(
        "Invalid gas fee ({}). valid range: [{}, {}].",
        gas_fee, config.min_btc_gas_fee, config.max_btc_gas_fee
    )
);
``` [1](#0-0) 

This check is called from `check_withdraw_psbt_valid`, which is invoked by `create_btc_pending_info` during the `ft_on_transfer` withdrawal flow: [2](#0-1) 

The same constraint is also applied in `check_psbt_output_all_change_address`, which is used by the cancel-withdraw and active UTXO management paths: [3](#0-2) 

The `withdraw_rbf` function — the user-accessible mechanism to bump a stuck transaction's fee — calls `check_withdraw_psbt` through `check_withdraw_rbf_psbt_valid`, applying the identical `max_btc_gas_fee` ceiling: [4](#0-3) 

`cancel_withdraw` is restricted to `Role::DAO` or `Role::Operator`: [5](#0-4) 

This creates a two-phase DoS:

**Phase 1 — New withdrawals blocked:** When Bitcoin mempool fees spike above `max_btc_gas_fee`, any call to `ft_transfer_call` → `ft_on_transfer` → `create_btc_pending_info` panics at the gas fee check. The NEP-141 callback failure causes the nBTC to be refunded, but the user cannot complete the withdrawal.

**Phase 2 — Existing pending withdrawals stuck:** A user who created a withdrawal when fees were within range (nBTC already deducted from their balance and held by the bridge) cannot RBF to a higher fee (same ceiling blocks it) and cannot self-cancel. Their nBTC is locked until the DAO either raises `max_btc_gas_fee` via `update_config` or calls `cancel_withdraw` on their behalf.

The `Config::assert_valid` only checks `min_btc_gas_fee < max_btc_gas_fee` with no lower bound on the ceiling: [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
**Medium.** This is a stuck bridge state requiring operator intervention. During Bitcoin fee spikes, pending withdrawals cannot be bumped via RBF (the only user-accessible escape hatch), and users cannot self-cancel. Their nBTC remains locked in the bridge contract until the DAO raises `max_btc_gas_fee` or manually cancels the withdrawal. No funds are permanently lost, but the bridge is operationally frozen for all affected withdrawals until governance acts.

### Likelihood Explanation
**Medium.** Bitcoin fee spikes are a recurring, well-documented phenomenon (Ordinals/Runes inscription waves, post-halving congestion, mempool storms). The gap between a typical configured `max_btc_gas_fee` and peak-congestion fee rates can be an order of magnitude. The scenario where a user's withdrawal is created at a low-fee moment and then fees spike before the transaction is confirmed is realistic and has occurred repeatedly on mainnet.

### Recommendation
1. Widen the `max_btc_gas_fee` ceiling to accommodate realistic fee-spike scenarios, or replace the hard ceiling with a softer advisory limit.
2. Allow users to self-cancel a pending withdrawal (returning nBTC) without requiring DAO/Operator intervention, so they are not dependent on governance response time during fee spikes.
3. Consider a user-supplied `max_gas_fee` parameter (already present in `TokenReceiverMessage::Withdraw`) as the binding ceiling for that specific withdrawal, rather than a global protocol ceiling, so individual users can opt into higher fees.

### Proof of Concept
1. Bitcoin mempool fee rate spikes to 500 sat/vbyte (common during inscription waves).
2. Alice calls `ft_transfer_call` on nBTC with a `Withdraw` message specifying UTXOs and outputs. The computed `gas_fee` for a typical 2-input/2-output transaction at 500 sat/vbyte is ~500,000 satoshis.
3. `create_btc_pending_info` → `check_withdraw_psbt_valid` → `check_withdraw_psbt` panics: `"Invalid gas fee (500000). valid range: [10000, 50000]."` (using the reference config values from `unit/mod.rs`).
4. Alternatively, if Alice's withdrawal was created at 30,000 sat gas fee (within range), and fees then spike, she calls `withdraw_rbf` with a new output reflecting 500,000 sat gas fee — same panic, same ceiling.
5. Alice cannot call `cancel_withdraw` (DAO/Operator only). Her nBTC is locked until the DAO acts. [7](#0-6) [1](#0-0)
