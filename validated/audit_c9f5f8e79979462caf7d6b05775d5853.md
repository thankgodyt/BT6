**However, the impact classification is wrong.**

The attacker must supply a **valid BTC Merkle proof** for a real, confirmed deposit. They are not minting nBTC without a backing deposit — the user's nBTC is minted correctly. What the attacker captures is the **relayer fee** (a fraction of the deposit bridge fee) by being the first to submit the proof.

This is a **front-running / open-relayer fee-capture** issue, not unauthorized minting or fund theft. The allowed impact categories that apply are:

- **Medium — Bypass of bridge limits or policies**: the policy that relayer fees should compensate authorized relayers is bypassed.

It does **not** meet Critical thresholds because:
- No user funds are lost or locked.
- No nBTC is minted without a real BTC deposit.
- No bridge verification is bypassed (the Merkle proof is still fully validated).
- The BTC backing ratio is unaffected.

The `relayer_white_list` and `confirmations_delta` mechanism show the protocol intends a distinction between trusted and untrusted relayers, but the fee-routing logic never enforces that distinction — it unconditionally uses `env::signer_account_id()`.

### Title
Unprivileged caller can capture relayer fee by calling `verify_deposit_v2` — (`contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/nbtc/mint.rs`)

### Summary
`internal_mint_promise` passes `env::signer_account_id()` as the `relayer_account_id` to `nbtc.mint`. Because `verify_deposit_v2` does not block non-whitelisted callers (only increases required confirmations), any account that submits a valid deposit proof first receives the relayer fee.

### Finding Description
`verify_deposit_v2` is decorated with `#[trusted_relayer]`, but that macro only adds `confirmations_delta` for non-whitelisted predecessors — it does not revert the call. Any account can therefore call `verify_deposit_v2` with a valid BTC inclusion proof and become the effective relayer. Inside `internal_mint_promise`, `env::signer_account_id()` (the attacker) is forwarded to `nbtc.mint` as `relayer_account_id`, so the nbtc contract credits the relayer portion of the deposit fee to the attacker rather than to a whitelisted relayer. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
**Medium.** Legitimate relayers lose their expected fee compensation. Users still receive correct nBTC amounts; the BTC backing is unaffected. The attacker gains only the relayer fee (a small fraction of the deposit amount), but systematic front-running could drain all relayer revenue and disincentivize authorized relayers from operating.

### Likelihood Explanation
Moderate. The attacker must monitor BTC deposits and submit valid Merkle proofs before the legitimate relayer. With `confirmations_delta > 0`, a non-whitelisted caller must wait for more confirmations, giving whitelisted relayers a head start — but this is not a hard block, and the window can be exploited.

### Recommendation
Replace `env::signer_account_id()` in `internal_mint_promise` with `env::predecessor_account_id()` (the direct caller of `verify_deposit_v2`), and add an explicit whitelist check that reverts the call if the predecessor is not a whitelisted relayer. Alternatively, accept a `relayer_account_id` parameter that is validated against the whitelist before being forwarded to `nbtc.mint`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy the bridge with a non-zero `relayer_fee` in `deposit_bridge_fee`.
2. Have a legitimate BTC deposit confirmed on-chain with enough blocks for `base_confirmations + confirmations_delta`.
3. Sign and submit `verify_deposit_v2` from an account **not** in `relayer_white_list`.
4. Observe that `VerifyDepositDetails` event emits `relayer_account_id = attacker` and the nbtc contract credits `relayer_fee` to the attacker.
5. The legitimate whitelisted relayer receives nothing for this deposit.
