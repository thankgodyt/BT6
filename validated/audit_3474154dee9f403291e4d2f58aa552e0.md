### Title
Per-Account Pending Withdrawal Limit Bypassable via Multiple Accounts, Enabling UTXO Pool Exhaustion - (File: `contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/account.rs`)

---

### Summary
The bridge enforces a per-account limit on simultaneous pending withdrawal (sign) transactions via `pending_tx_limits`, defaulting to 1. Because this check is scoped strictly to a single `AccountId`, an attacker holding nBTC across multiple NEAR accounts can bypass the limit entirely, submitting one pending withdrawal per account and locking all available UTXOs in the `unavailable_utxos` pool. This prevents legitimate users from initiating withdrawals until the attacker's transactions are resolved.

---

### Finding Description

`get_max_pending_sign_txs` reads a per-account cap from `pending_tx_limits`, falling back to `1` when no entry exists: [1](#0-0) 

The enforcement in `create_btc_pending_info` checks only the calling account's own `pending_sign_count()`: [2](#0-1) 

There is no global cap on total pending sign transactions across all accounts, and no limit on how many accounts a single actor may control. An attacker who distributes nBTC across N accounts can submit N simultaneous withdrawal requests, each consuming UTXOs from the shared pool. UTXOs consumed by `generate_vutxos` are moved to `unavailable_utxos` and are unavailable to any other withdrawal until the pending transaction is verified or cancelled: [3](#0-2) 

The `ContractData` struct confirms that `pending_tx_limits` is keyed per `AccountId` with no protocol-wide counter: [4](#0-3) 

---

### Impact Explanation
If the attacker's N accounts collectively consume all available UTXOs, no further withdrawals can be initiated by any user until the attacker's pending transactions are either verified on-chain or cancelled via operator intervention (`cancel_withdraw`). This constitutes an attacker-triggered temporary locking of bridged funds and a bypass of the bridge's per-account pending-transaction policy.

**Impact: Medium** — matches "Bypass of bridge limits or policies, or attacker-triggered temporary locking of bridged funds."

---

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires the attacker to legitimately hold nBTC (obtained by depositing real BTC or acquiring nBTC on the open market) and to control multiple NEAR accounts, which is trivially cheap on NEAR. The number of accounts needed equals the number of available UTXOs in the pool, which is a finite and observable on-chain quantity. The attack is fully scriptable and has no privileged prerequisites.

**Likelihood: Medium** — low cost, observable target state, no special access required.

---

### Recommendation
Introduce a global cap on total pending sign transactions (or total UTXOs locked in pending state) in addition to the per-account limit. Alternatively, enforce a minimum nBTC amount per withdrawal large enough to make UTXO exhaustion economically prohibitive, or implement a cooldown between withdrawals per account. The per-account limit alone is insufficient because it does not bound the aggregate system-wide UTXO lock-up.

---

### Proof of Concept

1. Attacker acquires nBTC and distributes it across K NEAR accounts (`attacker_0`, …, `attacker_{K-1}`), where K equals the number of available UTXOs in the bridge's pool.
2. Each account calls `ft_transfer_call` on the nBTC contract with a `Withdraw` message, routing to `ft_on_transfer` → `create_btc_pending_info`.
3. For each account, `account.pending_sign_count() < 1` passes (count is 0), so the withdrawal is accepted and one UTXO is moved to `unavailable_utxos`.
4. After K such calls, `utxos` is empty.
5. Any legitimate user attempting a withdrawal now finds no available UTXOs and the transaction reverts.
6. The attacker can sustain the lock by repeatedly initiating RBF (`withdraw_rbf`) on their pending transactions, resetting the `max_btc_tx_pending_sec` clock and delaying operator cancellation. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5)
