### Title
Unenforced `refund_address` in `DepositMsg` Allows Attacker to Redirect Stuck-Deposit Refunds to Arbitrary BTC Address — (`contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/deposit_msg.rs`, `contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/refund.rs`, `contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/api/bridge.rs`)

---

### Summary

The `refund_address` field in `DepositMsg` is optional. When a user deposits BTC without pre-authorizing a `refund_address`, the bridge's `request_refund` entry point accepts any caller-supplied BTC address as the refund destination with no ownership check. After the `unsafe_refund_timelock_sec` elapses, `execute_refund` (callable by anyone) will route the user's BTC to that attacker-controlled address.

---

### Finding Description

`DepositMsg` declares `refund_address` as `Option<String>`: [1](#0-0) 

`internal_request_refund` only validates the caller-supplied `refund_address` against the deposit message when the field is `Some`. When it is `None`, the check is skipped entirely and the caller's arbitrary address is stored verbatim: [2](#0-1) 

The stored `refund_address` is later used unconditionally to build the Bitcoin output that pays the refund: [3](#0-2) 

The public `request_refund` entry point contains no caller-identity check when `gas_fee` is `None`; the only role gate is for the optional custom fee: [4](#0-3) 

`execute_refund` is similarly unrestricted — any account may call it once the timelock has elapsed: [5](#0-4) 

The bridge does apply a longer `unsafe_refund_timelock_sec` when `refund_address` was not pre-authorized, explicitly to give DAO/Operator time to reject suspicious requests: [6](#0-5) 

This is a mitigation, not a fix. If the DAO/Operator window is missed, the attacker's request becomes executable.

---

### Impact Explanation

A user who deposits BTC without a `refund_address` in their `DepositMsg` has no on-chain guarantee that a refund will return to them. An attacker who observes a stuck deposit (one never finalized via `verify_deposit`) can:

1. Reconstruct the public `deposit_msg` from the on-chain deposit address derivation.
2. Call `request_refund` with their own BTC address and `gas_fee = None`.
3. Wait for `unsafe_refund_timelock_sec`.
4. Call `execute_refund` — the bridge's MPC pipeline signs and broadcasts a Bitcoin transaction paying the attacker's address.

The user's BTC is permanently lost. This matches **Critical — Significant loss or theft of user funds** if the DAO/Operator fails to reject within the timelock, and **Medium — attacker-triggered temporary locking** if the DAO/Operator is active and rejects in time.

---

### Likelihood Explanation

- The deposit address and the `deposit_msg` hash path are derived deterministically and are observable on-chain; an attacker needs no privileged information.
- Stuck deposits (e.g., due to relayer failure, light-client lag, or user error) are an expected operational scenario — the entire refund subsystem exists to handle them.
- The `unsafe_refund_timelock_sec` window is finite and may be missed during periods of operator inactivity, upgrades, or incidents.
- No NEAR tokens beyond the storage deposit are required to submit the attack.

Likelihood: **Medium**.

---

### Recommendation

Enforce the presence of `refund_address` in `DepositMsg` at deposit-address derivation time (i.e., in `get_deposit_path` / `verify_deposit` entry points), or reject `request_refund` calls where `deposit_msg.refund_address` is `None` unless the caller is the original `recipient_id` or a privileged role. At minimum, document explicitly that omitting `refund_address` transfers custody of the refund destination to whoever first calls `request_refund`.

---

### Proof of Concept

1. Alice deposits 0.1 BTC to the bridge address derived from `DepositMsg { recipient_id: "alice.near", refund_address: None, … }`.
2. The relayer fails to submit `verify_deposit`; the deposit is stuck.
3. Bob (attacker) observes the stuck UTXO on-chain, reconstructs `deposit_msg`, and calls:
   ```
   request_refund(
     deposit_msg = { recipient_id: "alice.near", refund_address: None, … },
     refund_address = "bc1q<bob_address>",
     tx_bytes = <Alice's deposit tx>,
     vout = 0,
     proof = <valid inclusion proof>,
     gas_fee = None
   )
   ```
4. `internal_request_refund` skips the address check (line 154–158 of `refund.rs`) because `deposit_msg.refund_address` is `None`; Bob's address is stored.
5. `resolve_execute_refund_timelock` returns `unsafe_refund_timelock_sec` (longer window, but finite).
6. After the timelock, Bob calls `execute_refund(utxo_storage_key)`.
7. The bridge constructs a PSBT paying `bc1q<bob_address>`, requests an MPC signature, and broadcasts the transaction.
8. Alice's 0.1 BTC (minus gas fee) is sent to Bob. [2](#0-1) [1](#0-0)
