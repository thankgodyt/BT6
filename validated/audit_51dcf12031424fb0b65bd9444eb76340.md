### Title
`chain_signatures_account_id` Cannot Be Updated After Deployment — (File: `contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/config.rs`)

### Summary
The `Config` struct stores `chain_signatures_account_id` — the address of the MPC signing contract used for every withdrawal and refund. `ConfigUpdate`, the only DAO-accessible mechanism to mutate live configuration, omits this field entirely, and no dedicated setter exists anywhere in the bridge API. Once deployed, the MPC contract address is permanently frozen. If the MPC infrastructure migrates to a new contract address, all withdrawal and refund signing operations will be permanently broken, locking all bridge-controlled BTC/ZEC.

### Finding Description
`Config` declares `chain_signatures_account_id` as a required field: [1](#0-0) 

Every withdrawal and refund PSBT is signed by calling this contract via the chain-signatures module. The DAO updates live configuration exclusively through `update_config`, which applies a `ConfigUpdate` struct: [2](#0-1) 

`ConfigUpdate` lists every mutable field — but `chain_signatures_account_id` is absent: [3](#0-2) 

`ConfigUpdate::apply` iterates only the fields it knows about via `set_if_some!` macros, and `chain_signatures_account_id` is not among them: [4](#0-3) 

No dedicated setter for `chain_signatures_account_id` exists anywhere in `management.rs` or `bridge.rs`. Searching the entire API surface confirms the field is write-once at `new()` and permanently frozen thereafter.

### Impact Explanation
Every withdrawal and refund goes through the MPC signing pipeline, which calls `chain_signatures_account_id`. If the MPC infrastructure migrates to a new contract (a realistic operational event — security upgrades, key rotation, infrastructure changes), the bridge has no mechanism to redirect signing calls. All pending and future withdrawals will fail at the signing step. Users who have already transferred nBTC/nZEC to the bridge to initiate a withdrawal will be unable to receive their BTC/ZEC back, and the bridge-held UTXOs become permanently inaccessible. This constitutes permanent locking of all bridge-controlled funds — a Critical-class outcome — or at minimum a stuck bridge state requiring a full contract upgrade to resolve.

### Likelihood Explanation
MPC infrastructure is actively evolving on NEAR. The chain-signatures service has undergone contract migrations in the past and will likely do so again. The probability that `chain_signatures_account_id` will need to change over the bridge's operational lifetime is non-trivial. No attacker action is required; the trigger is a routine infrastructure event. The missing setter is a structural gap that will be hit the first time the MPC contract address changes.

### Recommendation
Add `chain_signatures_account_id: Option<AccountId>` to `ConfigUpdate` and include it in `ConfigUpdate::apply` via the existing `set_if_some!` macro, mirroring how `btc_light_client_account_id` and `nbtc_account_id` are already handled. This gives the DAO a safe, auditable path to update the MPC contract address without requiring a full contract upgrade.

### Proof of Concept

1. Bridge is deployed with `chain_signatures_account_id = "v1.signer.near"`.
2. NEAR MPC infrastructure migrates; the canonical signing contract moves to `"v2.signer.near"`.
3. DAO calls `update_config` with every relevant field — but `chain_signatures_account_id` is not a field in `ConfigUpdate`, so the call cannot update it.
4. No other public or privileged function accepts a new `chain_signatures_account_id`.
5. All subsequent `execute_refund` and withdrawal signing calls target the decommissioned `"v1.signer.near"`, which no longer responds, causing every signing promise to fail.
6. Users who transferred nBTC/nZEC to initiate withdrawals cannot recover their funds; bridge UTXOs are permanently locked.

The structural gap is confirmed by the absence of `chain_signatures_account_id` in `ConfigUpdate`: [3](#0-2) 

compared to its presence as a non-optional field in `Config`: [5](#0-4)

    pub fn apply(self, config: &mut Config) {
