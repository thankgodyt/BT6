### Title
Relayer Removal Without Settling Pending Fee Claims Causes Permanent Fee Loss - (`near/omni-bridge/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary

The `claim_fee` function is gated by the `#[trusted_relayer]` macro. When a relayer is removed from the trusted set (via `resign_trusted_relayer` or `reject_relayer_application`) while they have pending fee claims in `pending_transfers`, those fees become permanently unclaimable. Neither the removal functions nor the protocol provide any mechanism to settle outstanding fee claims before deactivation.

### Finding Description

The `claim_fee` entry point enforces the `#[trusted_relayer]` guard:

```rust
#[payable]
#[trusted_relayer]
#[pause(except(roles(Role::DAO)))]
pub fn claim_fee(&mut self, #[serializer(borsh)] args: ClaimFeeArgs) -> Promise {
``` [1](#0-0) 

Inside `claim_fee_callback`, the fee is only paid to the original `fee_recipient`, and only the `fee_recipient` itself can call the function:

```rust
require!(
    fee_recipient == *predecessor_account_id,
    BridgeError::OnlyFeeRecipientCanClaim.as_ref()
);
``` [2](#0-1) 

A relayer earns a fee by calling `fin_transfer` (which stores a `TransferMessage` in `pending_transfers` with the relayer as fee recipient) or by performing a fast transfer via `fast_fin_transfer_to_other_chain` (which stores both a `FastTransfer` entry and a `TransferMessage`). The relayer must subsequently call `claim_fee` with a destination-chain proof to collect. [3](#0-2) 

The removal functions (`resign_trusted_relayer` / `reject_relayer_application`) perform no check for outstanding `pending_transfers` entries where the relayer is the fee recipient, and no settlement step is executed before deactivation. Once the relayer is no longer trusted, the `#[trusted_relayer]` guard on `claim_fee` permanently blocks them from collecting. There is no alternative code path to claim fees without being a trusted relayer. [4](#0-3) 

### Impact Explanation

For non-deployed (locked) tokens: the fee amount remains locked inside the bridge contract forever, constituting a direct permanent loss of bridged funds. For deployed (mintable) tokens: the relayer's earned fee is never minted, and the `pending_transfers` entry persists indefinitely. In both cases the relayer suffers a loss of funds they legitimately earned by executing bridge operations. The `pending_transfers` storage entries also accumulate permanently.

### Likelihood Explanation

Two realistic paths trigger this:

1. **Voluntary resignation**: A relayer calls `resign_trusted_relayer` without first claiming all pending fees (e.g., unaware of in-flight transfers, or resigning quickly after a batch of `fin_transfer` calls before the destination-chain proofs are ready).
2. **DAO revocation**: The DAO calls `reject_relayer_application` on an active relayer (confirmed by the test `test_dao_revoke_active_relayer`) without checking for outstanding fee claims. The DAO receives the stake but the relayer's pending fees are frozen. [5](#0-4) 

Both paths are reachable by unprivileged actors (the relayer themselves, or the DAO acting on a legitimate revocation).

### Recommendation

Before removing a relayer from the trusted set, the protocol should either:

1. **Require zero pending fee claims**: Check that no `pending_transfers` entries exist where the relayer is the fee recipient, and revert if any are found.
2. **Settle pending claims first**: Automatically process outstanding fee claims for the relayer before deactivation (analogous to the Blend mitigation of calling `gulp_emissions` before `remove_pool`).
3. **Decouple fee claiming from trusted status**: Allow a former relayer to call `claim_fee` for transfers where they are the recorded `fee_recipient`, even after losing trusted status, by checking `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id` independently of the `#[trusted_relayer]` guard.

### Proof of Concept

1. Relayer R is a trusted relayer.
2. R calls `fin_transfer` for a cross-chain transfer; a `TransferMessage` is stored in `pending_transfers` with R as fee recipient.
3. R calls `resign_trusted_relayer` (or DAO calls `reject_relayer_application` on R). R is removed from the trusted set and receives their stake back.
4. R attempts to call `claim_fee` with the destination-chain proof.
5. The `#[trusted_relayer]` macro rejects the call because R is no longer trusted.
6. No other account can call `claim_fee` for this transfer (the `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id` check blocks it).
7. The fee tokens remain permanently locked in the bridge; the `pending_transfers` entry is never removed. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6)
