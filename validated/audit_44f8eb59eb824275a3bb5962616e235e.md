Audit Report

## Title
Resigned Trusted Relayer Permanently Loses Earned Fast-Transfer Fees Due to `#[trusted_relayer]` Guard on `claim_fee` — (`near/omni-bridge/src/lib.rs`)

## Summary

`claim_fee` is the sole mechanism for a relayer to collect fees from fast transfers whose destination is a non-NEAR chain (EVM, Solana, etc.). The function is gated by `#[trusted_relayer]`, which panics if the caller is not currently in the trusted-relayer set. `resign_trusted_relayer` immediately removes the relayer from the trusted set with no check for outstanding fee claims, and `claim_fee_callback` additionally enforces `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id`, blocking any third-party recovery. The result is that fee tokens (e.g., USDC) fronted by the relayer are permanently frozen inside the bridge contract.

## Finding Description

**Fast-transfer-to-other-chain fee flow:**

When a relayer executes a fast transfer to a non-NEAR destination via `fast_fin_transfer_to_other_chain`, the full token amount (principal + fee) is transferred to the bridge contract. `burn_tokens_if_needed` burns only `amount_without_fee`; the fee portion remains in the contract's balance. Two storage entries are created: a `FastTransfer` record in `fast_transfers` and a `TransferMessage` with `origin_transfer_id = Some(fast_transfer.transfer_id)` in `pending_transfers`. [1](#0-0) 

The fee is only disbursed when the relayer later calls `claim_fee` with the EVM/Solana finalization proof. `claim_fee_callback` removes the `pending_transfers` entry, verifies the fast transfer is finalized, computes `fee = transfer_message.amount.0 - denormalized_amount`, and calls `send_fee_internal`. [2](#0-1) 

**The guard:**

`claim_fee` is decorated with `#[trusted_relayer]`, which rejects any caller not currently in the trusted-relayer set. The impl-level attribute exposes `bypass_roles(Role::DAO, Role::UnrestrictedRelayer)`, but a resigned relayer holds neither role. [3](#0-2) 

**The resignation path:**

`resign_trusted_relayer` (exercised in integration tests) immediately removes the relayer from the trusted set and returns their stake. There is no check for outstanding entries in `pending_transfers` or `fast_transfers` where the caller is the recorded fee recipient. [4](#0-3) 

**No third-party recovery:**

`claim_fee_callback` enforces `fee_recipient == *predecessor_account_id`, so no other account can call `claim_fee` on behalf of the resigned relayer. [5](#0-4) 

**No alternative withdrawal path:**

The `pending_transfers` entry created by `fast_fin_transfer_to_other_chain` has no alternative removal path reachable by the relayer after resignation. The fee tokens remain in the contract indefinitely. [6](#0-5) 

## Impact Explanation

This constitutes permanent freezing of bridged funds (e.g., USDC, wETH) inside the bridge contract. The fee tokens are real bridged assets that the relayer fronted; after resignation they have no reachable code path to recover them. This matches the critical impact class: *permanent freezing of bridged funds* and *fee mis-accounting that changes user or protocol balances*.

## Likelihood Explanation

Any trusted relayer can trigger this unilaterally by calling `resign_trusted_relayer` after executing a fast transfer to a non-NEAR destination but before calling `claim_fee`. The window between fast-transfer execution and fee claim can span hours or days (the destination chain must finalize first, and the first leg EVM→NEAR proof must also be submitted). A relayer may resign for entirely legitimate reasons (e.g., exiting the protocol) without realizing pending fees are forfeited. No external coordination or privileged access is required beyond the relayer's own keypair.

## Recommendation

In `resign_trusted_relayer`, before removing the relayer's trusted status, either:
1. Revert if the relayer has any `pending_transfers` entries where `origin_transfer_id` is set and the associated `FastTransferStatus.relayer` matches the caller, or
2. Relax the `#[trusted_relayer]` guard on `claim_fee` to also permit callers who are the `fee_recipient` recorded in the submitted proof, regardless of current trusted-relayer status (i.e., check proof-derived identity rather than live registry membership).

Option 2 is preferable as it does not block legitimate resignation and handles both self-resignation and DAO-revocation paths.

## Proof of Concept

1. Relayer stakes ≥ required minimum and becomes trusted.
2. Relayer calls `ft_transfer_call` on the token contract with a `FastFinTransferMsg` targeting an EVM address. `fast_fin_transfer_to_other_chain` runs: burns `amount_without_fee`, creates a `FastTransfer` entry, and inserts a `TransferMessage` with `origin_transfer_id` into `pending_transfers`.
3. The first leg (EVM → NEAR) is finalized via `fin_transfer` → `process_fin_transfer_to_other_chain`: the relayer receives `amount_without_fee` back and the fast transfer is marked finalized.
4. Relayer calls `resign_trusted_relayer`. Stake is returned; relayer is removed from the trusted set. The `pending_transfers` entry (containing the fee) is untouched.
5. The EVM destination finalizes the transfer and emits a `FinTransfer` event with `fee_recipient = relayer`.
6. Relayer calls `claim_fee` with the EVM proof. The `#[trusted_relayer]` macro panics — relayer is no longer trusted.
7. Any third party calling `claim_fee` is rejected by `fee_recipient == predecessor_account_id`.
8. The fee is permanently locked in the bridge contract.

A minimal integration test can reproduce this by: (a) setting up a trusted relayer, (b) executing `fast_fin_transfer_to_other_chain`, (c) calling `resign_trusted_relayer`, (d) attempting `claim_fee` and asserting it panics, (e) asserting the `pending_transfers` entry still exists via `get_transfer_message`.
