### Title
User-Controlled `gas_fee` Allows Zero-Fee Refund, Bypassing Bridge Fee Policy — (File: contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/refund.rs)

### Summary
The refund system accepts a caller-supplied `gas_fee` parameter with no minimum value enforcement. An unprivileged user can pass `gas_fee: Some(0)` to obtain a full refund with no gas fee deducted, forcing the bridge to create a zero-miner-fee Bitcoin transaction that will not confirm without operator intervention and requires the bridge to absorb the on-chain cost via RBF.

---

### Finding Description

In `request_refund_callback` (`contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/refund.rs`, lines 549–553), the gas fee is resolved and validated as follows:

```rust
let resolved_gas_fee = gas_fee.unwrap_or_else(|| self.get_refund_gas_fee());
require!(
    resolved_gas_fee < amount,
    "Gas fee must be less than deposit amount"
);
```

The **only** validation is `resolved_gas_fee < amount`. There is no minimum gas fee check. A caller who passes `gas_fee: Some(0)` satisfies `0 < amount` for any positive deposit amount. The refund request is then stored with `gas_fee = 0`. [1](#0-0) 

When `execute_refund` is subsequently called, `refund_execution_inputs` computes the refund amount as:

```rust
let refund_amount = refund_request
    .amount
    .checked_sub(refund_request.gas_fee)
    .expect("Deposit amount too small to cover gas fee");
require!(refund_amount > 0, "Refund amount is zero after gas fee");
```

With `gas_fee = 0`, `refund_amount = amount`. The bridge constructs a Bitcoin transaction whose single output equals the full deposit value, leaving **zero satoshis** as the miner fee. [2](#0-1) 

The `gas_fee` field flows from the user-supplied argument in `internal_request_refund` through to the stored `RefundRequest` without any floor check: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

---

### Impact Explanation

The bridge creates a valid but zero-fee Bitcoin transaction (inputs = outputs = deposit amount). Bitcoin miners will not prioritize or confirm a zero-fee transaction under normal mempool conditions. The deposit UTXO is now spent in an unconfirmed transaction, locking it until the bridge operator intervenes via Replace-By-Fee (RBF) or Child-Pays-For-Parent (CPFP). The operator must pay the miner fee from the bridge's own UTXO pool. An attacker can repeat this across multiple deposits to force the bridge to continuously absorb Bitcoin transaction costs and temporarily lock deposit UTXOs.

**Matched impact class:** Medium — Bypass of bridge limits or policies, or attacker-triggered temporary locking of bridged funds.

---

### Likelihood Explanation

Any user who has sent BTC to a valid deposit address can trigger this. The attack requires only:
1. A valid deposit transaction and inclusion proof.
2. Passing `gas_fee: Some(0)` to `request_refund`.

No privileged role, leaked key, or external dependency compromise is needed. The attack is straightforward and repeatable.
