### Title
No Incentive for Third-Party Refund Execution Leaves Stuck BTC Deposits Dependent on Operator Bots - (File: `contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/api/bridge.rs`, `contracts/satoshi-bridge/src/refund.rs`)

### Summary
The `execute_refund` entrypoint is permissionless but provides no economic reward to the caller. The executor must attach 1 NEAR which is explicitly non-refundable and acts only as an anti-spam fee. The entire refund amount flows to the original depositor's `refund_address`, and the `gas_fee` covers only the Bitcoin miner fee. If the team's operator bots fail, users who lack NEAR tokens have no path to recover their stuck BTC deposits, and no third party has any incentive to act on their behalf.

### Finding Description
When a BTC deposit fails to be finalized via `verify_deposit` (e.g., relayer downtime, wrong `deposit_msg`), the recovery path is:

1. `request_refund` — requires 2 NEAR attached, explicitly non-refundable.
2. `execute_refund` — requires 1 NEAR attached, explicitly non-refundable.

The `execute_refund` function carries no access control beyond the global pause: [1](#0-0) 

The attached deposit requirement is confirmed non-refundable by design: [2](#0-1) 

Inside `resolve_execute_refund_timelock`, the check enforces the 1 NEAR payment but returns nothing to the caller: [3](#0-2) 

In `internal_execute_refund` (Bitcoin path), the full `refund_amount` (deposit minus miner gas fee) is sent to `refund_address` — the original depositor — and the caller receives nothing: [4](#0-3) 

The `refund_amount` is computed as deposit minus `gas_fee`, with `gas_fee` going to the Bitcoin miner, not the NEAR executor: [5](#0-4) 

The project documentation confirms the intended operator-dependent model: "After timelock passes, DAO/Operator executes the refund": [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
If the team's operator bots fail, pending `RefundRequest` entries accumulate in `refund_requests` with no third-party actor economically motivated to process them. A user who deposited BTC but has no NEAR tokens cannot call `execute_refund` themselves (requires 1 NEAR, non-refundable). No external party will pay 1 NEAR for zero return. The user's BTC deposit is locked in the bridge's MPC-controlled UTXO set indefinitely, requiring operator intervention to recover. This matches the allowed impact: **stuck bridge state requiring operator intervention** and **temporary/permanent locking of user funds**.

### Likelihood Explanation
The team explicitly relies on their own bots as the primary execution path. Bot failure is a realistic operational risk (infrastructure outage, key rotation issues, monitoring gaps). The scenario is further compounded for users who are BTC-native and hold no NEAR, which is a common profile for bridge users depositing from the Bitcoin side. The timelock window (default `refund_timelock_sec` = 2 days, `unsafe_refund_timelock_sec` = 14 days) means stuck funds can persist for weeks: [7](#0-6) 

### Recommendation
Allocate a small portion of the BTC `gas_fee` (or a separate executor fee deducted from the refund amount) as a reward paid in nBTC or NEAR to the account that successfully calls `execute_refund`. This creates a permissionless market of third-party executors analogous to liquidation bots in lending protocols, removing the single point of failure on the team's own infrastructure. Alternatively, refund the 1 NEAR storage deposit to the executor upon successful `verify_refund_finalize` to at minimum make execution cost-neutral.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice deposits 0.01 BTC to her bridge address. The relayer is down; `verify_deposit` is never called.
2. Alice (a BTC-native user with no NEAR) cannot call `request_refund` or `execute_refund` — both require non-refundable NEAR deposits.
3. A third party observes the pending refund request via `get_refund_requests_paged`. They would need to pay 1 NEAR to call `execute_refund` and receive zero compensation. They decline.
4. The team's operator bot is offline. No one executes the refund.
5. Alice's BTC remains locked in the bridge's MPC UTXO set. The `RefundRequest` sits in `refund_requests` indefinitely: [8](#0-7) 

Recovery requires the team to restore their bot or manually intervene as `RefundOperator`/`DAO`, confirming the stuck-state-requiring-operator-intervention impact.

```rust
    pub(crate) fn internal_execute_refund(
        &mut self,
        utxo_storage_key: String,
        timelock_sec: u64,
        _chain_specific_data: Option<ChainSpecificData>,
    ) -> PromiseOrValue<()> {
        let refund_request = self.load_refund_request_for_execute(&utxo_storage_key, timelock_sec);
        let RefundExecutionInputs {
            outpoint,
            deposit_output,
            refund_amount,
        } = self.refund_execution_inputs(&refund_request);
        let refund_output = self.build_refund_output(&refund_request.refund_address, refund_amount);

        let mut psbt = PsbtWrapper::new(vec![outpoint], vec![refund_output]);
        psbt.set_input_utxo(vec![deposit_output]);

        let caller = env::predecessor_account_id();
        self.finalize_refund_with_psbt(
            caller,
            refund_request,
            psbt,
            refund_amount,
            utxo_storage_key,
        );
        PromiseOrValue::Value(())
    }
```
